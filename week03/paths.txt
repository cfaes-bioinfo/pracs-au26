# Paths and working directories {background-color="silver"}

## Terminology and concepts

- "**Directory**" (or "dir") is another term for *folder*
- A "**working dir**" is the dir you're currently located in
- Unix-based systems (MacOS, Linux -- OSC uses Linux) have a single
  starting point: the **root directory**, `/`

## What is a "path"?

- A "**path**" gives the location of a file or dir, with dirs separated
  by forward slashes (`/`)
- A leading `/` is the root dir; subsequent `/`s separate dirs

. . .

<hr style="height:1pt; visibility:hidden;" />

For example: `/fs/ess/PAS3493` --
`PAS3493` is inside `ess`, which is inside `fs`, which is in the root dir.

. . .

::: callout-tip
_When you code, you always use paths to refer to files and dirs._
:::

## Absolute versus relative paths

|  | Absolute paths | Relative paths |
|---|-------|-------|
| Meaning       | Start from the computer's root dir | Start from your working dir
| Recognized by | A leading `/` | No leading `/`
| Examples      | `/fs/ess/PAS3493` |  `data/meta/metadata.tsv` <br> `todo_list.txt`
| Analogy       | GPS coordinates: valid regardless of where you are | "Take the second left": depends on your current location

: {.striped .hover}

## Why prefer relative paths?

<details class="details-quiz">
<summary>Absolute paths don't depend on your working dir -- so what could be a disadvantage of using them?</summary>

Absolute paths:

- Don't generally work across computers
- Break when you move your entire project dir

</details>

. . .

::: callout-tip
Relative paths keep working after moving project dirs -- **as long as you
consistently use the project's top-level dir as your working dir**.
:::
